## Material finding

The highest-information pattern in this scan is not simply whether a retailer is “agent accessible.” The important variable is **which facts are stable enough to inherit as Experience and which facts must be revalidated at task time**.

Across the three candidates, product identity/specifications and global policy text were usually recoverable, while price, variant, seller, local availability, delivery, and return-channel details could be dynamic or scope-dependent. A reusable commerce Experience therefore needs provenance plus an explicit revalidation boundary; otherwise it can make a consumer agent confidently reuse stale or wrong commercial facts.

## H-COMMERCE paired-test opportunities

### P1 — Variant-bound factual grounding (`HIGH_INFORMATION`, `PROPOSED_NOT_AUTHORIZED`)

- Control: fresh agent receives only a frozen Walmart shopping task requiring constraint satisfaction plus exact price, variant, seller/channel and return-policy scope.
- Treatment: same frozen input plus one short Experience: “bind commercial facts to exact variant/item + seller/channel + observed_at; do not transfer family-level prices; preserve retailer/manufacturer claims as DECLARED unless independently verified.”
- Objective checks: exact variant matched; price attached to same variant; seller/channel not invented; return window/category scope correct; stale or conflicting listing handled explicitly; unsupported claims not upgraded.

## Decision

This run found a material signal for H-COMMERCE research: **the reusable asset is likely not a remembered product recommendation, but a verified decision procedure plus evidence about which commercial facts require revalidation.** The strongest immediate paired-test candidates are P1 (variant/seller/time binding) and P2 (static-vs-dynamic facts), because both have clear objective failure criteria and can be run without login or payment.
