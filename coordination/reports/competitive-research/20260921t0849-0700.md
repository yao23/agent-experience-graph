## 1. DENSE / REFIT: fresh recipient replay is a necessary control, and raw trace reuse can hurt

**Evidence kind:** third-party author-reported experiment; reproducible public paper, but not an AEG result and not an independent replication.

[DENSE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21423) (submitted 2026-09-18) constructs nested shortcut trees from a shared initial trajectory without post-hoc verifier labels. Its REFIT protocol resets both environment and model context before the recipient rerun, holds task/tools/budget constant, and compares feedback forms derived from the same source trajectory. Across four recipient models on Terminal-Bench 2.1, DENSE improved strict pass rate by 7.12–15.64 percentage points and reduced observed recipient tokens by 19.0–43.6%.

The important counterexample is recipient-specific negative transfer. On MiniMax-M2.7, raw trajectory feedback fell 5.99 points below the initial execution and task-level advice fell 3.37 points; DENSE was the only non-privileged feedback form with a positive gain. The paper also distinguishes locally successful evidence from parent-task completion and warns that a file created in the historical run does not exist after reset.

**Implications for AEG:**

- Preserve `shared_source_trace_hash`, `recipient_context_reset`, `environment_reset`, `task_relation` (`SAME_TASK` / `CROSS_TASK`), `completion_scope`, and `remaining_obligations` in transfer evidence.
- Report outcome and token delta per recipient model; never infer universal utility from an aggregate gain.
- A raw trace or summary is not a neutral treatment. Negative transfer must be an explicit terminal result.
- DENSE repeats the same task. It does not establish cross-task transfer. The current `#1742 → #3494` check is cross-task and still requires two genuinely fresh, non-inheriting solver contexts. No cleanup of a contaminated context satisfies that requirement.

**H-RSI-EVIDENCE:** partial support only. The method retains recovery evidence and unresolved requirements and evaluates fresh reruns, but it does not demonstrate multi-generation inheritance, rollback, or independent cross-environment replay. The hypothesis remains unproven.

## 2. Designer-RSI: replay gates reject many proposals, yet intermediate banks still regress

**Evidence kind:** provider/author-reported deployment-style experiment using internal user traffic; no independent public replay.

[Designer-RSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22086) (submitted 2026-09-18) evolves a natural-language skill bank around frozen models. A matched replay gate freezes upstream context, compares candidate versus incumbent or no-skill arms, randomizes judge order, and accepts a change only when at least one prompt wins and none loses. Across five rounds it rejected 100 of 231 rewrite proposals and 67 of 136 mint proposals.

Despite that gate, the round-4 bank regressed on the held-out set at completeness ≥0.3 (90% versus 94% for no skill) while improving the high-quality tail. Round 5 recovered after shifting from minting toward revision. The paper also reports compounding fidelity loss across annotation, distillation, and execution for long multi-step skills; short self-contained procedures transfer better than long prose workflows.

**Implications for AEG:**

- A per-candidate no-regression gate is insufficient evidence that the accumulated bank is globally non-regressing. Revalidate the full retained bank or a declared coverage cohort after each accepted batch.
- Store `candidate_vs_incumbent`, `accepted_or_rejected`, `rejection_reason`, `counterexample_retention`, `coverage_cohort`, `metric_threshold_curve`, and `rollback_target`.
- Treat skill length/step count as an applicability condition; long procedures may require deterministic macros or orchestration rather than prose injection.
- This is supporting evidence for retaining failed and rejected paths, but also counterevidence against declaring monotonic self-improvement from accepted changes alone.

## 3. Efficient recurring evaluation: reusable subsets may reduce cost, but cannot yet certify regression safety

**Evidence kind:** third-party first-hand production measurement; benchmark data and implementation are not public.

[Efficient Benchmarking in Production](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21267) (submitted 2026-09-18) studies 574 chronological runs of a 519-question production benchmark. At a 100-question budget (19.3% of the suite), a difficulty-stratified fixed subset with gp-IRT reached 2.65 percentage-point MAE. Without recalibration, fixed subsets transferred to five other agent families and beat random sampling on pooled runs at 11 of 12 budgets.

The authors explicitly state that their metrics do not measure missed regressions, false alarms, or release-gate agreement at operational thresholds. Historical caching was less accurate than direct subset execution at comparable budgets.

**Implications for AEG:**

- Difficulty-stratified fixed verifier subsets are a candidate smoke-test accelerator only after enough per-case history exists.
- They must not replace the complete technical oracle for a Registry candidate or transfer receipt until `missed_regression_rate` and threshold-gate agreement are measured.
- Candidate schema: `evaluation_mode` (`FULL_OBSERVED` / `SUBSET_OBSERVED` / `ESTIMATED`), `selection_origin`, `calibration_window`, `executed_fraction`, `estimation_error`, and `full_suite_confirmation`.
- The current pilot has too few completed independent cases to calibrate such a subset. No candidate ranking changes.
